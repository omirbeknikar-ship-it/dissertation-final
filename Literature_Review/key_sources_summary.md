# Key Sources Summary

This file summarizes the main strands of literature that will guide the final review. It does not include fabricated bibliographic entries. Verified citations should be added later after the final source list is confirmed.

## 1. BRI and Trade Facilitation

The first strand of literature treats BRI as a trade facilitation and connectivity project. In this view, infrastructure investment, logistics corridors, customs cooperation, rail links, and policy coordination reduce transaction costs and make cross-border trade easier. For Kazakhstan, this perspective is especially relevant because the country is geographically positioned as a transit and resource-exporting economy between China and wider Eurasian markets.

The strength of this literature is that it explains why trade volume might increase after BRI. It helps connect policy initiatives to measurable trade flows. Its limitation is that it often treats increased trade as a positive outcome without asking whether the smaller partner's trade balance improves.

## 2. BRI and Uneven Trade Outcomes

The second strand emphasizes uneven outcomes. It argues that connectivity can deepen existing asymmetries when one country exports raw materials and imports higher-value manufactured goods. In this interpretation, BRI can expand trade while also reinforcing dependence on the larger economy's demand, capital, logistics systems, and manufacturing capacity.

This literature is useful for the Kazakhstan-China case because it directs attention to the structure of trade rather than only the scale of trade. Its limitation is that it can sometimes assume dependency without testing whether specific export categories improve the smaller partner's trade position.

## 3. Kazakhstan-China Trade Structure

The third strand focuses on Kazakhstan-China trade composition. It generally highlights Kazakhstan's role as an exporter of energy, metals, minerals, and other resource-based goods, while China supplies manufactured goods, machinery, electronics, industrial inputs, and consumer products.

This strand is directly relevant because the research question concerns Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance, not total trade volume. The key issue is whether Kazakhstan's export structure has enough value to offset imports from China. The literature on trade structure also motivates the focus on strategic minerals as a specific export category rather than treating all exports as equivalent.

## 4. Strategic Minerals and Trade Position

The fourth strand concerns strategic minerals. Uranium, copper, and other critical or strategic mineral products can strengthen a country's export position because they are linked to energy security, industrial production, and long-term demand. For Kazakhstan, this matters because resource endowments may create export leverage in relations with China.

At the same time, strategic mineral exports do not automatically imply a stronger trade position. Their effect depends on prices, volumes, processing level, contract structures, and the scale of imports. This strand therefore supports the project's empirical question: whether mineral exports are associated with an improved bilateral trade balance, and whether this association changed after BRI.

## Working Synthesis

The four strands produce a clear analytical tension. BRI trade facilitation literature expects connectivity to increase trade. Dependency-oriented literature warns that increased trade may reproduce asymmetry. Kazakhstan-China trade structure literature shows why this question matters empirically. Strategic mineral literature provides the mechanism through which Kazakhstan might improve its trade position despite structural asymmetry.

# Theoretical Framework

## Overview

This project uses Dependency Theory and asymmetrical interdependence as the primary theoretical framework. Trade facilitation and connectivity under BRI serve as a supporting framework. The two perspectives are combined because the research question asks not only whether trade increased after BRI, but whether Kazakhstan's trade position improved.

## Dependency Theory and Asymmetrical Interdependence

Dependency Theory focuses on unequal economic relationships between stronger and weaker economies. In this project, the relevant concern is not colonial dependence in a narrow historical sense, but the broader problem of asymmetrical trade structure. Kazakhstan is a resource-rich economy with significant mineral and energy exports. China is a much larger economy with strong manufacturing capacity, diversified exports, and major demand for raw materials.

From this perspective, Kazakhstan-China trade may be asymmetrical in three ways:

1. Kazakhstan may rely heavily on resource-based exports.
2. China may export more diversified and higher-value manufactured goods.
3. Kazakhstan's export revenue may depend on commodity prices and Chinese demand.

The theory therefore warns that increased trade after BRI may not automatically improve Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance. Trade expansion can coexist with dependency if the structure of exchange remains uneven.

## Trade Facilitation and Connectivity under BRI

The trade facilitation perspective emphasizes the practical mechanisms through which BRI can expand trade. These include transport corridors, logistics infrastructure, customs coordination, border efficiency, policy coordination, and improved market access. Kazakhstan's geographic position makes this framework especially relevant because the country is central to overland connectivity between China and Eurasia.

This perspective expects BRI to reduce barriers to trade and increase flows of goods. It does not, by itself, determine whether Kazakhstan's trade balance improves. Trade facilitation can increase exports, imports, or both. Therefore, it is best used as a mechanism for explaining trade expansion rather than as a complete theory of trade balance outcomes.

## Integration of the Two Frameworks

The integrated framework is based on a simple distinction:

```text
Trade facilitation explains why trade may increase.
Dependency theory explains why increased trade may remain uneven.
```

BRI may improve transport and connectivity, but the effect on Kazakhstan's trade balance depends on the structure of trade. If Kazakhstan's exports to China grow through strategic mineral exports, the bilateral trade balance may improve. If imports from China grow faster or if Kazakhstan remains concentrated in low-value resource exports, the trade balance may not improve.

Strategic mineral exports are therefore the key bridge between the theories. They are resource exports, which fits the dependency concern, but they may also have high strategic and economic value, which creates potential trade-balance benefits. The project tests whether this potential is visible in Kazakhstan-China annual trade data.

## Theoretical Expectations

The framework produces three expectations:

1. BRI is expected to be associated with increased bilateral trade activity.
2. Increased trade activity is not necessarily expected to improve Kazakhstan's trade balance.
3. Strategic mineral exports may improve Kazakhstan's trade balance, but this effect may depend on the post-BRI period.

## Implications for Research Design

The dependent variable should be trade balance, not total trade volume. The key explanatory variables should include a post-BRI period indicator, strategic mineral export measures, and an interaction between the two. The interpretation should remain cautious because the theory identifies plausible mechanisms, not automatic causal proof.

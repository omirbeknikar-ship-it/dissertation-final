# Examiner-Style Evaluation (final validation)

## Verdict

The thesis asks one question about one outcome and answers it with evidence that is kept in three categories (accounting identities, descriptive comparisons, statistical associations). It makes no causal claim. Its conclusions are stated as ranges over specifications and limited to merchandise trade. The contribution is modest and specific: a two-sided assessment of whether the change in Kazakhstan–China merchandise trade was distinctive, and a mechanism-by-mechanism check of whether it matches the BRI's observable predictions. Provisional assessment: **pass, likely good**, if the candidate can state precisely what "China-specific", "not Kazakhstan-specific" and "inconsistent with a mechanism" mean and do not mean.

## Ten difficult questions, with answers the thesis can support

**1. Why the bilateral balance? A deficit is not a problem.**
Agreed, and the thesis does not treat it as one (§1.3, §7.4). The balance is the registered outcome and an accounting identity: explaining a USD 15.5 bn swing requires accounting for every export and import change, so it organises the analysis of the whole merchandise relationship. Turnover is reported alongside it. Conclusions are limited to merchandise trade; the thesis does not assess welfare, investment or transit.

**2. Why is the BRI relevant if you find no BRI-timed change?**
Because the change is China-specific from Kazakhstan's side (52–92 per cent of the swing), the BRI is the obvious candidate and had to be examined. The thesis does not conclude that the BRI had no effect; it concludes that the merchandise-trade evidence does not show a change that both matches a BRI mechanism and starts or accelerates at a BRI milestone (§7.1–7.2).

**3. Why start in 2013? The BRI was announced then, not implemented.**
2013 is the announcement; it was also the peak year of oil exports to China. The thesis therefore (a) reports all comparisons over four base periods (2011–13 average, 2012, 2013, 2014) and four end periods (Table 6.3b); (b) re-runs the timing test with breaks at dated milestones, 2012 (Altynkol–Khorgos rail crossing), 2015 (Khorgos dry port) and 2017 (Khorgos stake, road loans) (Table 6.9b). Completion dates of most projects could not be verified (Table 6.13b), so lagged effects cannot be excluded.

**4. What does "China-specific" mean?**
The part of the change that would not have occurred had Kazakhstan's trade with China in each product grown at the rate of its trade in that product with the rest of the world (§5.1). It measures distinctiveness, not causation, and it is not a bound on any BRI effect, because the BRI can also change trade with other partners.

**5. Are the comparison groups appropriate? Russia was sanctioned; Kyrgyzstan is a re-export hub; the countries differ in size and composition.**
Partly. Growth factors normalise for size (Table 6.10c). Results are reported for six comparator sets and two weightings, with and without Russia and Kyrgyzstan (Table 6.10b). The only result that holds for every neighbour separately is for 2019–2024: China's exports grew 2.2–3.2× to each neighbour and 2.20× to Kazakhstan. Over 2013–2024 the result depends on the comparator (Kazakhstan grew faster than Tajikistan and Turkmenistan), and the thesis says so. The neighbours are BRI members and affected by post-2022 re-routing, so the comparison shows the post-2019 import rise is regional, not why.

**6. What do the robustness checks establish?**
That three statements hold across all specifications: most of the swing is China-specific relative to Kazakhstan's other partners; strategic minerals offset part of it; agri-food reoriented towards China without acceleration at the milestones tested. They also show which statements do not hold robustly, and these were removed or narrowed (the 92 per cent headline, "≈0 net", "under every comparator", "mostly after 2019" for minerals).

**7. Does the theory predict what you observe?**
The trade-cost literature predicts more trade, especially in time-sensitive goods; it does not predict a balance direction (Table 2.1). In the windows tested, Kazakhstan–China turnover did not expand specifically before 2019 (contrary to a Kazakhstan-specific M1 expansion); agri-food rose as M2 predicts in products but did not accelerate; energy fell, contrary to M3. The average effects in the literature (a few per cent) are too small for this design to detect.

**8. How do you separate the BRI from Chinese demand, commodity prices and other shocks?**
Commodity prices and Kazakhstan-wide shocks are removed by the product-by-year comparison with Kazakhstan's other markets; regional shocks by the comparison with China's neighbours. China's own demand, bilateral policy and BRI channels cannot be separated (§5.6). That is why no causal claim is made.

**9. What do the statistical results mean? Is "no acceleration" evidence against the BRI?**
The agri-food rise is statistically robust (Holm p < 0.001; larger than all 300 random chapter sets). For agri-food, the trend change is negative at every break year and the one-sided p for acceleration is 0.96–0.99: this is evidence against the specific prediction of an acceleration at those milestones, not evidence that the BRI had no effect. For metals and other goods, the estimates are imprecise: absence of evidence. Minimum detectable trend changes are 0.16–0.56 log points per year depending on group and break year (Tables 6.9, `trend_break_alternative_years.csv`).

**10. What are the data limits, and what is the contribution?**
HS-2 aggregation; imports from other partners only as totals; China's chapter-level exports to Kazakhstan for three years; Kazakhstan's 2020–2022 partner data incomplete; energy one chapter; merchandise only; AidData commitments only; project completion dates mostly unverified. The contribution is a transparent, two-sided assessment for one country pair, showing which parts of the change in Kazakhstan–China merchandise trade were distinctive, from whose side, and how each relates to the observable predictions of BRI mechanisms; plus documentation of the 2020–2022 reporting gap. It does not estimate a BRI effect.

## Remaining weaknesses

- No causal identification; China's demand and BRI channels are confounded.
- Comparators are BRI members and affected by re-routing; whole-period import result is comparator-dependent.
- Project completion dates and lags unknown.
- Aggregation (HS-2; import totals).
- Several analyses exploratory (§3.3), including the alternative break years and new comparators added in the final validation.

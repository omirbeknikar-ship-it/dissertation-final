# The Thesis in Plain English (under 250 words)

Since the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) was launched in Astana in 2013, Kazakhstan has gone from selling China USD 3.5 billion more than it buys to buying USD 12 billion more (2024).

A deficit is not automatically bad. Imports can be machinery for investment or cheaper goods, and the BRI's benefits, such as transit fees for China–Europe trains, may not show up in goods trade at all. So I use the balance as a way to account for everything that changed in goods trade, not as a measure of gain or loss.

My question: was this change special to Kazakhstan's relationship with China, and does it look like what the BRI was supposed to do?

What I found:

- Compared with Kazakhstan's trade with other countries, most of the change (52–92 per cent, depending on years compared) was specific to China.
- The biggest piece, a jump in imports after 2019, also happened to China's other neighbours. In 2019–2024 Kazakhstan's imports from China grew the slowest of the six countries.
- Oil sales to China fell more than elsewhere, the opposite of the BRI's resource logic.
- Strategic minerals partly offset the swing.
- Food exports shifted towards China, which fits the BRI's market-access idea, but the shift started before 2013 and didn't speed up after any BRI milestone.

Conclusion: the change is real and mostly China-specific, but the goods-trade evidence doesn't match the BRI mechanisms I could test. That does not mean the BRI had no effect.

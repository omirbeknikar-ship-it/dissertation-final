# Abstract

This paper examines whether the post-Belt and Road Initiative period is associated with a change in Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance with China, and whether strategic mineral exports changed their relationship with that balance. The theoretical frame is asymmetric interdependence: Kazakhstan and China can both gain from lower trade costs, but benefits may be uneven if Kazakhstan's export position remains concentrated in minerals while imports from China expand across higher-value goods. The analysis builds an annual panel for 2000-2024, with usable trade-balance observations through 2023, from local Comtrade, WITS, World Bank, Pink Sheet, FRED, and AidData files. Descriptive statistics show that the average trade balance fell from USD 3.35 billion before BRI to USD 2.08 billion after BRI, while narrow mineral exports rose from USD 1.92 billion to USD 2.48 billion. Structural-break tests identify evidence around 2013-2014 but also a 2009 break. AIC-selected ARDL models find a negative post-BRI x minerals association in the headline dummy specification. Synthetic control and placebo DiD are not estimated because donor and partner-placebo annual bilateral panels are unavailable locally. Results are therefore associational, not causal.

# Introduction

Kazakhstan occupies a central position in the overland economic geography of China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI). The Silk Road Economic Belt was announced in Kazakhstan in 2013, and Kazakhstan's transport corridors, mineral exports, and financing relationships have since been folded into the wider language of Eurasian connectivity. The policy question is often stated in optimistic terms: more infrastructure should reduce trade costs, increase trade, and create opportunities for landlocked economies. The research problem in this paper is narrower and more demanding: did the post-BRI period coincide with an improvement in Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance with China, and did strategic mineral exports become more or less beneficial for that balance?

The distinction between trade growth and trade-balance improvement matters. A corridor can increase both exports and imports. It can also deepen commodity concentration if the smaller partner exports raw or semi-processed goods and imports machinery, consumer goods, and industrial inputs. Kazakhstan's mineral endowments give it assets that China values, but those assets do not automatically translate into a stronger external position. The effect depends on prices, import growth, exchange rates, and whether minerals are embedded in higher-value domestic linkages.

This paper uses asymmetric interdependence as the lead theory. The point is not to assume that BRI created dependency, nor to assume that connectivity was automatically beneficial. Interdependence becomes politically and economically asymmetric when one side is more vulnerable to disruption or has fewer outside options [@keohane_nye_1973; @hirschman_1945]. This lens fits Kazakhstan-China trade because China is a large market, financier, and supplier, while Kazakhstan is a smaller economy with a resource-heavy export basket.

The empirical strategy is deliberately transparent. Phase 1 constructs the annual panel from local files because the original API scripts required keys that were unavailable. Phase 2 estimates descriptive, structural-break, ARDL, synthetic-control feasibility, placebo-DiD feasibility, and robustness scripts. Phase 3 rewrites the literature review around asymmetric interdependence, gravity and BRI trade facilitation, resource dependence, and global value chains (GVCs). Phase 4, this paper, reports only numbers generated by scripts.

The main finding is cautious. The average bilateral trade balance is lower in the post-BRI period even though mineral exports are higher. Chow tests show breaks at 2013 and 2014, while multiple-break detection also finds a major 2009 break. The headline ARDL/OLS dummy model estimates a negative post-BRI x minerals coefficient. However, synthetic control and DiD cannot be estimated from the local data, and robustness checks include sign-reversing ARDL rows. The evidence supports an association between the post-BRI period and a weaker mineral trade-balance payoff; it does not establish a causal BRI effect.

# Context

Kazakhstan's trade relationship with China is shaped by geography, resource endowments, and the politics of corridor development. Kazakhstan is landlocked but strategically positioned between China, Russia, Central Asia, and Europe. BRI infrastructure narratives identify this geography as an opportunity: rail, road, energy, logistics, and border facilities can reduce time costs and connect inland production to larger markets. Gravity models predict that lower trade costs should increase bilateral exchange, but the distribution of gains depends on what each economy sells and buys.

Kazakhstan's exports to China are heavily resource-based. The fixed codebook basket defines narrow strategic minerals as uranium, copper, and chromium products, while the broad basket adds zinc, lead, titanium, and rare earth-related products. The local Comtrade files do not provide a full HS-6 annual panel for 2000-2024, so the processed data use available mineral proxies and mark HS-6 limitations explicitly. This is not a cosmetic issue: if the empirical object is strategic minerals, aggregation level affects interpretation.

The clean panel contains 25 annual rows for 2000-2024, but trade-balance observations are usable through 2023 because the 2024 import/trade-balance variables are missing. This limitation is recorded in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` and in `Scripts/03_build_panel.py`. Oil exports are missing in the clean panel, so the ARDL script excludes `oil_exports` and records `oil_exports_note = excluded_all_missing` in `Outputs/generated_tables/ardl_results.csv`.

The descriptive record already shows why the question is not simply whether Kazakhstan sold more to China. In the pre/post table generated by `Scripts/10_descriptives.py`, the average trade balance falls from USD 3.35 billion before BRI to USD 2.08 billion after BRI, a change of -37.84 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "Trade Balance (USD)"). The trade-balance ratio falls from 0.405 to 0.197, a change of -51.18 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "Trade Balance Ratio"). At the same time, narrow mineral exports rise from USD 1.92 billion to USD 2.48 billion, or 29.22 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "Minerals Narrow (USD)"). Broad minerals rise more sharply, from USD 1.92 billion to USD 3.61 billion, or 87.98 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "Minerals Broad (USD)").

These patterns motivate the core puzzle: mineral exports increased, but the bilateral balance did not improve on average. That does not prove that minerals harmed the balance. It suggests that the post-BRI trade structure may have combined export growth with faster import growth, price-cycle effects, or limited upgrading.

# Theory

The lead theoretical claim is that Kazakhstan-China trade should be treated as asymmetric interdependence. Keohane and Nye's interdependence framework emphasizes that economic links create sensitivity and vulnerability, not simply cooperation [@keohane_nye_1973]. Hirschman adds that foreign trade can generate influence when a partner's dependence is concentrated and costly to replace [@hirschman_1945]. The central question is therefore not whether trade exists, but whether Kazakhstan can adjust if the terms, prices, routes, or financing conditions shift.

Gravity and trade-facilitation theory explain why BRI could increase trade. Anderson and van Wincoop show that bilateral trade depends on trade costs relative to multilateral resistance [@anderson_vanwincoop_2003]. BRI transport projects can reduce shipping time and border costs, especially along corridors. de Soyres and coauthors estimate that BRI infrastructure can reduce shipment time and trade costs, while Baniya, Rocha, and Ruta estimate positive trade effects from the New Silk Road using gravity analysis [@desoyres_etal_2019; @desoyres_etal_2020; @baniya_rocha_ruta_2020]. Bird, Lebrand, and Venables show that Central Asian effects are spatially differentiated rather than uniform [@bird_lebrand_venables_2020].

Resource-dependence theory adds a second channel. Sachs and Warner link resource abundance to development risks when economies remain concentrated in primary commodities [@sachs_warner_1995]. van der Ploeg's review emphasizes that resource wealth can be a curse or a blessing depending on volatility, institutions, and reinvestment [@vanderploeg_2011]. Harvey, Kellard, Madsen, and Wohar show that primary commodity prices have long-run trends and breaks, so mineral-export values cannot be interpreted without price context [@harvey_etal_2010].

GVC theory supplies the development benchmark. Gereffi, Humphrey, and Sturgeon argue that governance structures shape whether suppliers upgrade or remain in captive, low-value positions [@gereffi_humphrey_sturgeon_2005]. For Kazakhstan, the favorable BRI scenario is not only more mineral exports; it is more processing, logistics capability, and downstream linkages. Recent work on Chinese state capital and resource-based transformation in Kazakhstan directly raises this question [@jepson_2024].

Dependency theory remains in the background. It is useful for thinking about unequal exchange, but this paper does not use it as the primary lens because it can obscure Kazakhstan's agency and the possibility of mutual gains. Asymmetric interdependence is more precise: it allows the same trade relationship to produce gains, risks, and bargaining asymmetries at once.

# Literature Review

The BRI trade literature generally expects infrastructure and trade-facilitation improvements to increase trade flows. The strongest econometric basis comes from gravity and GIS-linked models. de Soyres and coauthors quantify changes in shipping times and trade costs from BRI infrastructure [@desoyres_etal_2019; @desoyres_etal_2020]. Baniya, Rocha, and Ruta find that trade effects are larger when transport investment is paired with trade reforms [@baniya_rocha_ruta_2020]. These findings justify including BRI timing in a trade model, but they do not imply that a smaller partner's bilateral balance improves.

Central Asia studies caution that geography and local political economy matter. Bird, Lebrand, and Venables model how BRI can reshape economic geography in Central Asia, with gains unevenly distributed across locations [@bird_lebrand_venables_2020]. Kahraman's spatial empirical analysis similarly treats Central Asia and the South Caucasus as regions with spillover effects rather than homogeneous treatment units [@kahraman_2025]. Shah's Kazakhstan case study emphasizes implementation and perception dynamics [@shah_2018].

Kazakhstan-focused work also shows that BRI is not only an infrastructure program. Primiano and Kudebayeva's survey evidence finds uneven public reception in Kazakhstan [@primiano_kudebayeva_2025]. Mostowlansky's Khorgos study shows how border security, soft power, and suspicion interact at the Sino-Kazakh boundary [@mostowlansky_2020]. Brassett examines how BRI affects Sino-Kazakh transboundary water management beyond formal negotiations [@brassett_2023]. Petry shows that financial infrastructures are part of Chinese economic statecraft, not secondary to physical corridors [@petry_2023].

The resource and GVC literatures shift the question from volume to structure. Calabrese's Kyrgyzstan study asks whether Chinese capital can support diversification away from extractives [@calabrese_2024]. Jepson and Sweeney ask whether Chinese state capital can support downstream linkages in resource-based economies, including Kazakhstan [@jepson_2024]. These studies make the post-BRI x minerals interaction a theoretically meaningful coefficient: it is a compact test of whether mineral exports became more or less associated with Kazakhstan's bilateral external position after BRI.

# Data

The main dataset is `Collected_Raw_Data/clean_panel_annual.csv`, produced by `Scripts/03_build_panel.py`. It merges local Comtrade/WITS-derived trade measures, annual Brent and copper prices, World Bank GDP/exchange-rate/CPI variables, and AidData Chinese development finance variables. The unit is Kazakhstan-China year.

The panel has 25 rows for 2000-2024, but the econometric sample is 24 rows for 2000-2023 because 2024 lacks the import and trade-balance variables (`clean_panel_annual.csv`; validated in `Scripts/10_descriptives.py`, which filters `year < 2024`). The key dependent variables are `trade_balance_usd` and `trade_balance_ratio`. The key mineral variables are `minerals_narrow`, `minerals_broad`, and a WITS legacy ores/metals proxy used in robustness.

The price controls are Brent annual mean and copper annual mean. The Brent mean rises from 64.87 to 68.00 USD/bbl across the pre/post split, or 4.82 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "Brent Crude (USD/bbl)"). Copper rises from 5062.75 to 6875.51 USD/mt, or 35.81 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "Copper (USD/mt)"). These controls are important because mineral export values mix quantities and prices.

The BRI finance variable is `bri_intensity = log1p(cumulative Chinese finance commitments)`. Its mean rises from 20.99 to 24.44, or 16.43 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`, row "BRI Intensity (log1p)"). The post-BRI dummy follows the codebook convention: years 2000-2013 are pre, and years 2014 onward are post. Robustness varies the inclusive threshold across 2013, 2014, 2015, and 2016.

Data limitations shape the identification strategy. The local Comtrade files do not contain the donor-country bilateral trade-balance-with-China panels needed for synthetic control. They also do not contain annual KAZ-Russia, KAZ-EU, or KAZ-Turkey bilateral panels needed for within-country placebo DiD. `Scripts/13_synthetic_control.py` and `Scripts/14_did_placebo.py` therefore write skipped-status tables rather than estimates.

# Empirical Strategy

The first empirical step is descriptive. `Scripts/10_descriptives.py` produces pre/post summary statistics, time plots, and stationarity diagnostics. ADF, Phillips-Perron, and KPSS tests are reported in `stationarity.csv`. The short sample produces mixed integration classifications: trade balance, minerals, Brent, copper, China GDP, BRI intensity, and log minerals are classified as ambiguous; Kazakhstan GDP is classified as I(1) (`stationarity.csv`, `integration_order` column). This supports the use of ARDL-style models that can handle a mix of I(0) and I(1) regressors, while avoiding variables that appear I(2).

The second step tests structural breaks. `Scripts/11_structural_breaks.py` runs Chow tests at 2013-2016 and a Bai-Perron-style multiple-break procedure using binary segmentation because R `strucchange` is unavailable in the environment. Chow tests are significant at 2013, 2014, and 2015: the 2013 test has F = 10.4344 and p = 0.0008; the 2014 test has F = 6.6471 and p = 0.0061; the 2015 test has F = 2.8362 and p = 0.0823 (`structural_breaks.csv`, Chow rows). The 2016 Chow test is not significant at conventional levels, with F = 1.2017 and p = 0.3215 (`structural_breaks.csv`, 2016 Chow row). The multiple-break procedure selects 2009 and 2014, with bootstrap 95 percent intervals of 2004-2009 and 2014-2019 (`structural_breaks.csv`, Bai-Perron-style rows).

The third step estimates ARDL models in `Scripts/12_ardl.py`. The headline dummy specification uses trade balance in USD billions as the dependent variable and includes minerals, Brent, KZT/USD, log Kazakhstan GDP, log China GDP, `post_bri_2013`, and the post-BRI x minerals interaction. The alternative replaces the dummy with `bri_intensity`.

The fourth step attempts stronger identification designs. Synthetic control is skipped because requested donor-country bilateral series are unavailable (`synthetic_control_status.csv`, `status = DECISION NEEDED - skipped`). Within-country placebo DiD is skipped because local files do not contain annual KAZ-Russia, KAZ-EU, KAZ-Turkey, or KAZ-ROW bilateral trade-balance panels (`did_placebo_status.csv`, `status = DECISION NEEDED - skipped`). These skipped designs define the causal boundary of the paper.

The fifth step is robustness. `Scripts/20_robustness.py` estimates a grid over BRI start years, minerals definitions, outcomes, sample exclusions, and estimators. It writes every failure and skipped DiD row to `robustness.csv`.

# Results

The descriptive result is the first substantive finding. Mineral exports rise after BRI, but the average trade balance falls. The pre/post average trade balance decreases by 37.84 percent, while narrow minerals increase by 29.22 percent and broad minerals increase by 87.98 percent (`summary_stats_pre_post.csv`). This pattern is consistent with the possibility that import growth or changing trade composition offset mineral export gains.

The stationarity tests are mixed. Trade balance has ADF p = 0.2466, PP p = 0.9576, and KPSS p = 0.1000, producing an ambiguous classification (`stationarity.csv`, row "Trade Balance"). Minerals narrow has ADF p = 0.4668, PP p = 0.9169, and KPSS p = 0.1000, also ambiguous (`stationarity.csv`, row "Minerals Narrow"). Kazakhstan GDP is the only variable classified as I(1) in the table (`stationarity.csv`, row "KAZ GDP"). These results do not invalidate ARDL, but they do caution against overconfident inference in a 24-observation sample.

Structural-break evidence supports attention to the BRI period, but not a clean 2013-only break. Chow tests show breaks at 2013 and 2014. The multiple-break procedure selects 2009 and 2014. The 2009 break likely reflects the global financial crisis and commodity shock, while the 2014 break is closer to the first full post-announcement BRI year and also coincides with regional currency and commodity pressures. The paper therefore treats 2013-2014 as theoretically relevant but uses robustness checks across 2013-2016.

The headline ARDL/OLS dummy model estimates a negative interaction. In `ardl_results.csv`, Model A has `coef_interaction = -3.2205` and `pval_interaction = 0.0071`; the sample size is 24, R-squared is 0.7645, and adjusted R-squared is 0.6614. The AIC-selected ARDL long-run coefficient for `post_bri_2013_x_minerals_narrow_B` is -1.731751 with HAC SE 0.428019 and p = 0.0012 (`ardl_long_run_coefficients.csv`, Model_A_dummy interaction row). This means that, conditional on the model and scale, the post-BRI period is associated with a lower trade-balance payoff to each USD billion of narrow mineral exports.

The dummy model's bounds test does not establish cointegration. `ardl_results.csv` reports PSS F = 1.8830, upper-bound p = 0.5338, lower-bound p = 0.0924, and the script conclusion `NO COINTEGRATION` for Model A. This weakens long-run causal interpretation. The CUSUM test is stable at 5 percent, but CUSUMSQ is not stable (`ardl_results.csv`, Model_A_dummy).

The finance-intensity model points in the same short-run interaction direction but is less reliable. Model B has `coef_interaction = -2.4760` and `pval_interaction = 0.0240` in `ardl_results.csv`. Its bounds test is inconclusive, with PSS F = 2.2599, upper-bound p = 0.3093, and lower-bound p = 0.0310. The long-run multipliers in `ardl_long_run_coefficients.csv` are extremely large with very large HAC standard errors, such as -1963.075365 for minerals with HAC SE 29220.148146 and p = 0.9478. This is reported as an unstable long-run intensity specification rather than treated as evidence.

The identification scripts do not estimate causal effects. Synthetic control has no MSPE ratio and no placebo permutation because donor-pool trade-balance series are not available (`synthetic_control_status.csv`). Placebo DiD has no coefficient, no p-value, and zero observations because annual partner-placebo panels are absent (`did_placebo_status.csv`). These are not null results; they are data availability failures.

The robustness grid is mixed but informative. `robustness.csv` contains 288 rows. OLS estimates succeed in 96 rows, ARDL long-run estimates succeed in 94 rows and fail in 2 rows, and DiD is skipped in 96 rows (`robustness.csv`, status counts printed by `Scripts/20_robustness.py`). The headline robustness rows for start year 2014, narrow minerals, level outcome, and full sample match the main result: OLS interaction = -3.220476 with SE 1.042891 and p = 0.002015; ARDL long-run interaction = -1.731751 with SE 0.428019 and p = 0.001203 (`robustness.csv`, headline rows).

However, the robustness script also identifies 11 estimated OLS/ARDL rows where the interaction sign turns positive (`robustness.md`, "Sign-Reversing Estimated Rows"; counted by `Scripts/20_robustness.py`). These sign reversals occur mainly in ARDL long-run ratio specifications and alternative thresholds. This fragility matters. The headline level-result is not overturned in the preferred row, but the broader grid shows that conclusions are sensitive to outcome scaling, lag selection, and BRI timing.

# Discussion

The results are most consistent with asymmetric interdependence rather than a simple success or failure story. Kazakhstan's mineral exports to China increased after BRI, but the average bilateral balance weakened. The headline interaction suggests that the mineral export payoff to the trade balance was lower in the post-BRI period. This is consistent with a trade structure in which minerals alone do not compensate for import growth or broader dependence on China-facing supply chains.

At the same time, the evidence does not prove that BRI caused the weaker payoff. The 2009 structural break shows that Kazakhstan-China trade is also shaped by global shocks. The 2014 break may reflect BRI-related reorientation, commodity prices, exchange-rate shifts, or several forces at once. Without donor-country synthetic control or partner-placebo DiD, causal attribution remains out of reach.

The GVC interpretation is important. If BRI were supporting upgrading, one might expect mineral exports to translate more strongly into a favorable balance, or at least not less strongly. The negative headline interaction suggests a need to examine whether Kazakhstan's China-facing mineral trade is connected to domestic processing and downstream linkages. This aligns with the literature on Chinese state capital and resource-based transformation [@jepson_2024] and on diversification away from extractives in Central Asia [@calabrese_2024].

The finance-intensity results also caution against treating AidData commitments as a clean annual treatment. Chinese finance is lumpy, commitments do not equal disbursements, and project effects can occur with long lags. The unstable long-run intensity coefficients in `ardl_long_run_coefficients.csv` support using finance intensity as a robustness lens, not as the main identification variable.

# Policy Implications

For Kazakhstan, the policy implication is not to reduce trade with China, but to improve the structure of that trade. If mineral exports rise while the balance weakens, policy should focus on domestic value addition, processing, logistics services, and supplier capabilities. Strategic minerals create leverage only if Kazakhstan can retain more value and diversify the uses of export revenue.

Trade facilitation should be paired with industrial and competition policy. The BRI literature suggests that infrastructure gains are larger when complemented by border reforms and trade reforms [@baniya_rocha_ruta_2020]. For Kazakhstan, this means that corridor investment should be evaluated not only by tonnage or transit volume, but by whether it expands domestic firms' capabilities and bargaining options.

Data policy is also a policy implication. Kazakhstan-facing BRI evaluation requires partner-level, product-level, and project-level data. The inability to estimate synthetic control and placebo DiD in this project is itself evidence that public data infrastructure constrains policy evaluation.

# Limitations

The main limitation is data availability. The local Comtrade extracts are not the full HS-6 annual series requested in the original design. Oil exports are missing in the clean panel. The 2024 trade balance is missing. Donor-country bilateral trade-balance series and partner-placebo series are unavailable locally. These limitations prevent stronger causal designs.

The second limitation is sample size. Annual data for 2000-2023 produce 24 usable observations, which is small for dynamic models with multiple controls. ARDL lag selection helps discipline the model, but it cannot remove all small-sample fragility.

The third limitation is measurement. Mineral export values reflect both quantities and prices. Brent and copper controls help, but uranium price data are absent from the local price files. AidData commitments are not equivalent to realized project spending.

The fourth limitation is identification. The paper reports associations, break patterns, and robustness checks. It does not claim that BRI causally worsened or improved Kazakhstan's trade balance.

# Conclusion

This paper finds that the post-BRI period is associated with a weaker average Kazakhstan-China trade balance even as mineral exports increased. Structural-break tests support attention to 2013-2014 but also identify 2009 as a major break. The headline ARDL/OLS dummy model estimates a negative post-BRI x minerals interaction, meaning that mineral exports are associated with a lower trade-balance payoff after BRI in the preferred level specification. The finance-intensity model points in a similar short-run direction but has unstable long-run multipliers. Robustness checks preserve the headline result in the preferred row but reveal sign reversals in some ARDL ratio specifications.

The most defensible interpretation is that BRI-era connectivity coincided with deeper Kazakhstan-China trade integration without clear evidence of improved bilateral external position for Kazakhstan. This is an asymmetric-interdependence finding, not a causal dependency claim. The next empirical step is clear: obtain full HS-6 Comtrade data and donor/placebo bilateral panels, then re-estimate synthetic control and DiD.

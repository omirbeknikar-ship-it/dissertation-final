# Defence Script (final validated version; matches `defense_presentation.pptx`, 16 slides)

Target: about 15 minutes for slides 1–15; slide 16 is backup. All numbers are from the final manuscript.

---

## Slide 1. Title (20 s)
"My thesis is on the Belt and Road Initiative and the Kazakhstan–China trade balance, and the role of strategic mineral exports. I use UN Comtrade data reported by both countries, World Bank totals, AidData's records of Chinese official finance, and dated connectivity milestones."

## Slide 2. What happened (1 min)
"In China's data, Kazakhstan had a surplus of 3.5 billion dollars with China in 2013, a deficit of 3.9 billion in 2019 and of 12 billion in 2024: a swing of 15.5 billion. From 2013 to 2019 the swing came from energy exports, down 7.6 billion. From 2019 to 2024 it came from imports, up 15.2 billion, while metal and ore exports rose 5.6 billion. I use China's data because Kazakhstan's own partner data for 2020–2022 leave out copper, zinc, aluminium and machinery."

## Slide 3. The balance is not the relationship (1 min 15 s)
"The BRI is about trade costs, connectivity, investment and trade expansion, not about the balance. Lower trade costs can raise both exports and imports, so the balance can move either way. Nearly half of the import rise since 2019 is machinery, electrical equipment and vehicles, which can be investment. Transit fees from China–Europe trains are services and are not in goods trade at all.

So a deficit is not a loss, and I don't treat it as one. I use the balance because it is my registered outcome and because it is an identity: every export and import change is part of it, so explaining it means explaining the whole goods relationship. I also report turnover. My conclusions are about merchandise trade only."

## Slide 4. Puzzle and research question (1 min)
"Before-and-after comparisons can't separate the BRI from shocks to all of Kazakhstan's trade, like the commodity collapse, or shocks to all of China's regional trade, like re-routing after 2022. So my question is: is the post-2013 change in Kazakhstan–China merchandise trade, summarised by the balance, distinctive to the relationship, and is it consistent with the BRI mechanisms that can be observed in goods trade, especially through strategic minerals? I use two separate comparisons, judge timing against dated milestones, and make no causal claim."

## Slide 5. Literature and mechanisms (1 min 15 s)
"The literature finds positive BRI effects on trade costs and trade volumes: de Soyres et al. estimate cost reductions of 1.5 to 2.8 per cent; Baniya, Rocha and Ruta up to 4.1 per cent more trade, more for time-sensitive goods; Foo et al. a positive effect for ASEAN; Mau and Seuren higher European exports through rail. None of them studies a bilateral balance, so their findings are compatible with any balance direction, and effects of a few per cent are below what my data can detect.

From them I take five mechanisms. Trade costs predict more bilateral turnover. Market access predicts more agri-food exports, accelerating at milestones. Resource finance predicts more energy and minerals. Investment-linked imports and transit can't be tested with my data, and I say so."

## Slide 6. Three kinds of evidence (1 min)
"I keep accounting identities, descriptive comparisons and statistical tests separate. The comparisons show distinctiveness, not causes, and they are not a bound on BRI effects. Safeguards: four product groups fixed in advance, Holm correction, 300 random chapter sets, power, 16 base and end-year combinations, four break years and six comparator sets. Some analyses were added after I saw earlier results, and the thesis lists them."

## Slide 7. Result 1 (1 min)
"Compared with Kazakhstan's trade with its other partners, product by product, 52 to 92 per cent of the swing is China-specific depending on the years compared, 71 per cent with averaged endpoints. Its largest part is imports after 2019. And total trade with China did not expand specifically before 2019: the China-specific turnover component is between minus 0.9 and plus 2.0 billion."

## Slide 8. Result 2 (1 min 15 s)
"Was the import rise specific to Kazakhstan? Between 2019 and 2024 China's exports grew 2.2 times to Kazakhstan, and between 2.2 and 3.2 times to each of its five neighbours. Kazakhstan was the lowest of the six. So no Kazakhstan-specific mechanism is needed. Over the whole period, the answer depends on the comparator, because Tajikistan's and Turkmenistan's imports from China fell before 2019. I can't separate post-2022 re-routing, Chinese supply, or a region-wide or lagged effect of BRI connectivity."

## Slide 9. Result 3 (1 min)
"On exports: oil to China fell more than to other markets, mainly between 2013 and 2014, which is the opposite of the resource-finance expectation. Strategic minerals offset part of the swing in every specification, but their timing isn't tied to Chinese finance. Agri-food rose about twelvefold relative to other markets, and this is robust. Metals and ores are significant only with 2023–24."

## Slide 10. Result 4 (1 min 15 s)
"If a BRI mechanism drove the agri-food shift, the trend should speed up at a milestone. I tested four: the 2012 rail crossing, the 2013 announcement, the 2015 dry port and 2017. At each, the trend change is negative and the one-sided p-value for acceleration is 0.96 to 0.99. That is evidence against that specific prediction. It is not evidence that the BRI had no effect. For metals and other goods the estimates are too imprecise to say."

## Slide 11. Result 5 (45 s)
"Chinese official finance was 48 billion dollars in 2000–2013 and 16 billion in 2014–2021, mostly for pipelines, oil and copper before 2013 and roads and border posts after; none for agriculture. The rail crossing opened in 2012, the dry port in 2015. For most projects I could verify only announcement or financing dates, not completion, so lagged effects can't be excluded."

## Slide 12. Mechanism scorecard (1 min)
"Trade costs: no China-specific turnover rise to 2019, and the later rise is regional, so this is inconsistent with a Kazakhstan-specific expansion and inconclusive about regional or lagged effects. Market access: consistent in products, inconsistent with the predicted acceleration. Resource finance: inconsistent for energy, inconclusive for minerals. Investment-linked imports and transit: untested. No change both matches a mechanism and starts or accelerates at a milestone."

## Slide 13. Strategic minerals (1 min)
"For my registered topic: minerals offset part of the swing. China takes 80 per cent of Kazakhstan's ore exports but Kazakhstan supplies under 2 per cent of China's ore imports. Uranium reverses this: about two-thirds of China's natural uranium imports came from Kazakhstan in 2023. And dependence is not vulnerability: China's share of Kazakhstan's refined copper exports fell from 65 to 49 per cent in 2025."

## Slide 14. What is established (1 min)
"Established: the size and components of the swing, the China-specific share, no China-specific turnover rise to 2019, the regional import rise, the oil fall, the mineral offset, the agri-food shift without acceleration, the timing of finance, and the data gaps. Interpretation: that the evidence doesn't match the mechanisms in the windows tested. Untested: any causal effect, transit, investment, regional and lagged effects, welfare."

## Slide 15. Conclusion (45 s)
"The change in Kazakhstan–China goods trade is mostly China-specific from Kazakhstan's side; its largest part was shared by China's other neighbours; and no change both matches a BRI mechanism and starts or accelerates at a BRI milestone. That is not evidence that the BRI had no effect. For policy: don't read the deficit as a loss, monitor trade by component against both benchmarks, keep market access and border processing efficient, and reconcile national statistics with China's. Thank you."

## Slide 16. Backup
"92 per cent is the maximum of the grid; the range is 52 to 92, 71 with averages; Kazakhstan's own data give 92 for 2013–2024."

## Ten likely questions (short answers; full versions in `examiner_evaluation.md`)

1. **Why the balance?** Registered outcome and an identity that organises the whole goods relationship; not a welfare measure; turnover reported too.
2. **Why the BRI if no BRI-timed change?** The change is China-specific from Kazakhstan's side, so the BRI had to be examined; I conclude the goods-trade evidence doesn't match its observable mechanisms, not that it had no effect.
3. **Why 2013?** Announcement year and oil peak; so I use 16 base/end combinations and test breaks at 2012, 2015, 2017; completion dates mostly unverified.
4. **What does "China-specific" mean?** The part of the change that wouldn't have happened had trade with China grown like Kazakhstan's trade with the rest of the world, product by product. Not causal, not a bound.
5. **Are the comparators appropriate?** Partly; only the 2019–2024 result holds for every neighbour; over the whole period it depends on the comparator; neighbours are BRI members and affected by re-routing.
6. **What do the robustness checks show?** Which claims survive every specification and which don't; the latter were removed.
7. **Does theory predict your results?** It predicts more trade, not a balance direction; tested predictions (Kazakhstan-specific turnover rise, energy rise, agri-food acceleration) are not observed in the windows tested; average effects are too small to detect.
8. **How do you separate the BRI from other shocks?** World prices and regional shocks are removed by the comparisons; China's own demand and BRI channels cannot be separated; hence no causal claim.
9. **Is "no acceleration" evidence against the BRI?** Against that specific prediction at those milestones, yes; against the BRI as a whole, no.
10. **What is the contribution?** A transparent two-sided assessment of which parts of the change in Kazakhstan–China goods trade were distinctive, and how each relates to BRI mechanisms, plus documentation of the 2020–2022 data gap.

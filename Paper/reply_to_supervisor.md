**Why I changed the design**

The original thesis regressed the annual bilateral balance (n = 24) on mineral exports and a post-2013 dummy. Three things made it unreliable: a post-2013 dummy in one country pair cannot separate the BRI from the 2014–2016 commodity price collapse; the monthly interaction disappeared once oil exports were controlled for, and Granger tests showed that minerals do not lead the balance; and some data problems (a placeholder oil series and incomplete 2020–2022 import records) affected the results. Your point about the small sample is addressed by moving to product-level data: 97 HS-2 chapters × two destinations × 17 years (3,160 observations).

**Research question**

*Is the post-2013 change in Kazakhstan–China merchandise trade, summarised by the swing in the bilateral balance, distinctive to the Kazakhstan–China relationship, and is it consistent with the BRI mechanisms that are observable in merchandise trade, in particular through strategic mineral exports?*

I treat the balance as an accounting summary of the whole merchandise relationship, not as a welfare measure. A deficit can reflect investment imports, cheaper goods or transit. I report turnover alongside the balance, and I limit the conclusions to merchandise trade.

**Methods**

- An accounting decomposition of the swing, using China's reported data (Kazakhstan's partner data omit copper, zinc, aluminium and machinery in 2020–2022).
- Two separate descriptive comparisons:
  - with Kazakhstan's trade with its other partners, product by product, which removes world prices and Kazakhstan-wide shocks;
  - with China's trade with Russia and the Central Asian states, which removes regional shocks.

  Both are reported over 16 combinations of base and end years and several comparator sets.
- PPML triple-difference, event-study and trend-break models on product-level exports, with these safeguards:
  - Holm corrections;
  - randomisation inference with 300 random chapter sets;
  - minimum detectable effects;
  - breaks at dated milestones: 2012 (Altynkol–Khorgos rail crossing), 2013 (announcement), 2015 (Khorgos dry port) and 2017.
- AidData records of Chinese official finance, and a table of dated connectivity milestones.

No causal effect is estimated. I distinguish accounting identities, descriptive comparisons and statistical associations throughout.

**Main findings**

1. The swing in the balance (China-reported) was USD 15.5 billion, from +3.5 billion in 2013 to −12.0 billion in 2024. Relative to Kazakhstan's other partners, 52–92 per cent of it is China-specific depending on the years compared (71 per cent with averaged endpoints). Bilateral turnover did not expand specifically with China before 2019.
2. The largest component is the rise in imports from China after 2019. It was not specific to Kazakhstan: in 2019–2024 China's exports grew 2.2–3.2 times to each of Kazakhstan's five neighbours and 2.2 times to Kazakhstan. Over the whole period the comparison depends on the comparator.
3. Oil exports to China fell by more than to other markets, mainly in 2013–2014, which is contrary to the resource-security expectation.
4. Strategic mineral exports offset part of the swing in every specification. Their timing is not tied to Chinese resource finance, which peaked before 2013.
5. Agri-food exports reoriented towards China. This is statistically robust (Holm-adjusted p < 0.001, and larger than all 300 random chapter sets). It matches the BRI market-access mechanism in products, but it began before 2013 and shows no acceleration at any milestone tested.

**Contribution and limits**

The contribution is a two-sided assessment of whether the change in Kazakhstan–China merchandise trade was distinctive, and a mechanism-by-mechanism check of whether it matches the BRI's observable predictions. The conclusion is that the change is mostly China-specific from Kazakhstan's side and that its largest part was shared by China's other neighbours. The merchandise-trade evidence does not show a change that both matches a BRI mechanism and begins or accelerates at a BRI milestone. This is not evidence that the BRI had no effect: transit, investment, productivity and welfare are not measured, and region-wide or lagged effects cannot be excluded.

Main limitations:

- no causal identification;
- comparators are themselves BRI members and were affected by re-routing after 2022;
- completion dates of most projects could not be verified;
- data are at HS-2 level, with imports only as totals;
- several analyses were added after earlier results were seen, and the thesis states which.

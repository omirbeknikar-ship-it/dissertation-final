# Research Plan

## Title

BRI's Impact on Kazakhstan-China Trade Balance: The Role of Strategic Mineral Exports

## Background

Kazakhstan occupies an important position in the economic geography of the Belt and Road Initiative. It connects China to Central Asia, Russia, and Europe through rail, road, energy, and logistics corridors. Since the announcement of BRI in 2013, Kazakhstan-China trade has become more visible in policy discussions and regional economic analysis. However, growth in bilateral trade volume is not the same as improvement in Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance.

Kazakhstan exports a significant share of resource-based goods, including energy products and strategic minerals. China exports a broad range of manufactured goods, machinery, consumer products, and industrial inputs. This structure raises an important research problem: the same connectivity that increases trade may also reproduce asymmetrical trade relations if Kazakhstan remains primarily a supplier of raw or semi-processed materials while importing higher-value goods.

## Research Problem

The central research problem is that much discussion of BRI emphasizes increased connectivity, investment, and trade flows, but pays less attention to whether the trade position of the smaller partner improves. For Kazakhstan, the key question is not only whether trade with China increased after BRI, but whether the increase translated into a more favorable bilateral trade balance.

Strategic minerals are especially important because they may give Kazakhstan bargaining and export advantages. Uranium, copper, and related mineral products can generate export revenue and may improve Kazakhstan's trade balance with China. At the same time, reliance on strategic mineral exports may deepen dependence on commodity cycles, external demand, and limited value-added production.

## Research Question

Did the Belt and Road Initiative improve Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance with China, and did BRI change the effect of strategic mineral exports on that trade balance?

## Aim

The aim of this project is to evaluate whether the post-BRI period is associated with an improved Kazakhstan-China bilateral trade balance and to assess whether strategic mineral exports became more important for Kazakhstan's trade position after BRI.

## Objectives

1. Construct a transparent annual dataset on Kazakhstan-China bilateral trade using verified public sources.
2. Measure Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance with China in absolute and relative terms.
3. Identify strategic mineral export values and their share in Kazakhstan's exports to China.
4. Compare pre-BRI and post-BRI patterns in trade volume, export composition, and trade balance.
5. Estimate a parsimonious regression model suitable for a small annual sample.
6. Interpret findings through Dependency Theory, asymmetrical interdependence, and trade facilitation logic.

## Methodological Approach

The project will use an annual country-pair design. The unit of observation will be year. The main dependent variable will be Kazakhstan's bilateral trade balance with China, measured as exports from Kazakhstan to China minus imports from China to Kazakhstan. A secondary measure may use a trade balance ratio to reduce scale sensitivity.

The main explanatory variables will be a post-BRI indicator, strategic mineral exports, and an interaction between the two. The post-BRI indicator will mark years after the announcement of BRI in 2013. Strategic mineral exports will be constructed from verified commodity-level trade data after the relevant product categories are selected and documented.

Because the sample will be small, the empirical strategy will remain parsimonious. The model will not include a large number of controls. Any controls, such as Kazakhstan's GDP or exchange rate indicators, will be included only if they are theoretically justified and do not overburden the model.

## Expected Contribution

The expected contribution is conceptual and empirical. Conceptually, the project shifts attention from trade growth to trade balance and trade structure. Empirically, it proposes a transparent way to examine whether strategic mineral exports improve Kazakhstan's bilateral trade position with China in the post-BRI period.

The project is not designed to provide definitive causal proof. Its contribution is to produce a disciplined, reproducible, and theoretically grounded analysis that can support a final term paper and potentially be expanded into a larger research project.
